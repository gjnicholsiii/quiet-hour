What the Silence Says
3:37 A.M.8:12 A.M.
Joe Nichols · Quiet Hour

Quiet
Hour

In this quiet hour, when the world retreats into its fitful sleep and leaves you alone with thoughts and a page. It is three-thirty in the morning, perhaps four, when even God seems to have turned His face away, busy with other hemispheres, other sorrows. This is when I write best. A conspiracy between myself and the page, hidden from the eye that judges, and from you who might stumble upon these words and find them wanting.

Writing in the observed world is a performance, even when we pretend otherwise. There is always someone watching. The imagined reader, the critic perched like a vulture on our shoulder, the God who sees all things and finds them either worthy or damning. But at this hour, in this darkness, I am invisible even to myself. My fingers move across keys like a blind pianist playing a song no one will hear, and in that blindness, that deafness, that perfect isolation, truth emerges.

The darkness is not merely absence of light, it is presence of possibility. When I cannot see my own hand moving, even in the beckoning glow the screen, when even my own breathing seems too loud in the silence, then the words come unguarded. They steal themselves across the page like thieves in the night, sneaking past every defense I have built against honesty. They whisper the things I would never say in daylight, never admit to you. Perhaps, never confess even in prayer.

Here, in this sanctuary of shadows, I am powerful in ways that daylight strips from me. The morning will come with its harsh judgment, its insistence that all things be labeled and categorized and found either acceptable or wanting. But now, now I am king over language, emperor of my own small kingdom of secrets. I can write the words that would make my mother weep, that would make you turn away, that would make God, if He is listening, shake His head in that particular way reserved for His most disappointing children.

But perhaps that is the point. Perhaps the reason I write in darkness is not to hide from judgment but to remember what it feels like to be human without apology. In the daylight world, we curate our thoughts, each one polished and presented with little plaques explaining its significance, its appropriateness, its contribution to the greater narrative of who we pretend to be. But here, now, in this unobserved moment, I can scatter my thoughts like seeds in untended soil and see what wild things grow.

The power is intoxicating. To write without witnesses is to taste what Adam must have felt in that brief moment before he knew he was naked. The perfect freedom of existing without context, without comparison, without the weight of being seen. I am not writing for you in these moments, though you may read this. I am not writing for God, though He may be listening despite my attempts at hiding. I am writing for the pure animal pleasure of making marks that mean something, anything, everything.

But the irony. The irony. The moment I speak of this freedom, I destroy it. The moment these words leave the darkness and enter the light of your reading, they become performance again. They become observed. They become, in some sense, lies, because the truth can only exist in that space between the moment the thought forms and the moment it becomes language.

So I return, again and again, to the darkness. To the hour when sensible people sleep and only insomniacs and madmen and writers are awake to witness the world's secrets. I return to hide from you, to hide from Him, to hide even from myself long enough to remember what my voice sounds like when it is truly alone.

And what I find there, in those dark and unobserved moments, is that I am not asking forgiveness. I am not begging to be understood or loved or even acknowledged. I am simply declaring myself into existence, word by word, line by brutal line, and the darkness holds me like a mother holds a child.

This is what you cannot touch, what God cannot judge, what even I cannot edit once it lives on the page. This bleeding, human heart refusing to be reasonable. Here, I am not the person who smiles when spoken to, who nods in agreement, who swallows words to keep the peace. Here, I am feral with honesty, drunk on existence, and allowed to feel without explanation, to want without shame.

So come morning, when you need me to be who you can handle, when God waits for prayers that sound the way prayers are supposed to sound, I beg you remember this. Somewhere in the dark, I am writing all of it. The rage, sure, but also the bewildering moments of grace. I am writing fuck you to the voices that told me to be less, and thank you to those that applauded when I was more. I am writing forgiveness to those who couldn't love me the way I needed, and fury at the systems that taught them to fear me at my best. I am writing love letters to the people who remained when I exploded into honesty, and eulogies for the ones who fled. I am writing the truth about what your conditional love cost me, and also how it forged me into someone who refuses to love conditionally in return. The darkness holds it all, the gratitude and the grief, the forgiveness and the damnation, the tender recognition that even those who wounded me were probably scrawling their own desperate truths in their own stolen hours, trying to remember who they were before the world taught them to be afraid of their own voices.

Yes.

I am here. I am writing. And in the darkness, I can be more clearly seen than in any light.
